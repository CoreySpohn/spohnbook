## Lesson

The libraries model one physical chain: light from a star, a planet and dust crosses to an observer inside the local zodiacal cloud, passes a telescope and coronagraph, lands in the same detector pixels, and only then becomes records and an inferred orbit.

## What to say

The relay is five slides. Each keeps the previous slide's result on the left and adds one new thing on the right.

1. Relay step 1. On the left is one system seen side on: a star, a planet and a tilted dust disk, with the observer far to the right. The planet reflects starlight and the dust scatters it. On the right is the same system as the observer sees it on the sky, as a noiseless model: the dust ring, brighter on its near side, with the star and the planet marked.
2. Relay step 2. That sky scene is now on the left. Its light, three kinds of it, reaches a telescope that sits inside our own zodiacal dust, so a fourth, local, contribution joins it along the same line of sight. A generic coronagraph suppresses the starlight and ends on a detector. This is not a flight design.
3. Relay step 3. The instrument is now on the left. On the right is a synthetic raw frame of one visit, in which all four contributions land in the same pixels: leakage from the star, the planet, the exozodiacal dust and the uniform local zodiacal floor. The amplitudes are schematic. The frame has no compass on purpose: which way is north on the detector depends on the roll and on an image-coordinate decision that is still open.
4. Relay step 4. The frame is now on the left. On the right is the record that visit reduces to: an epoch, a reporting event, a measured offset with its covariance, and the calibration revision it was reduced with. The card lists what a record carries, not values.
5. Relay step 5. The record card is now on the left. On the right, positions from four visits constrain the orbit; the crosshairs are plus and minus one standard deviation, and the ringed position is the visit of the frame we just saw. Each pink track is one posterior draw. The draws agree where the visits are and fan out on the side of the orbit nobody has observed yet.
6. Full map. Everything on one slide. The top row is light, drawn as solid arrows. The bottom row is data, drawn as hollow arrows. The outlined tags name the chapter that defines each part: Geometry, Dust, Radiometry, Optics and Records.

## Assumptions

- Single scattering by optically thin dust; the planet's orbit lies in the disk midplane, which is a choice made for the picture, not a requirement.
- The side view uses the proposed observer-toward-positive-Z profile of the geometry chapter (`geometry-illumination`). Sky positions and the sky image follow the chapter's proposed observer profile (`geometry-observer-basis`): (X, Y, Z) = (north, east, toward observer), the node angle measured from north toward east, offsets ordered (east, north). orbix and skyscapes label the same rotated components right ascension and declination, so their outputs are mapped by exchanging those two components. The sky panels (the model sky and the inferred orbit) are shown east to the left and north up, with a compass. The raw frames carry no compass: how detector rows and columns map to east and north depends on the roll and on the pending image-coordinate decision (`optics-roll-rotation`).
- The telescope and coronagraph are generic. The raw frame is one Poisson realization of schematic amplitudes, not a simulation of an instrument, and carries no brightness ratio. The orbit draws are a posterior sample for the four measured positions. The prior takes eccentricity uniform on [0, 0.6], the argument of periapsis and the mean anomaly at epoch uniform on the circle, and the semimajor axis, inclination and node angle Gaussian about the simulated values (15 percent, 15 degrees, 15 degrees). Six million prior samples are weighted by the Gaussian likelihood of the positions, and thirty draws are taken by systematic resampling. Centering three of the priors on the simulated values is a choice made for the picture.
- The picture is an original schematic composition. It is not an identity, a convention or an instrument design.

## Source locators

- `geometry-illumination` in Geometry and time: observer toward positive Z, near and far side.
- Dust models chapter, "Rays, frames and angles": local zodiacal light and exozodiacal light as one scattering process seen from different places.
- `radiometry-response-ownership` and `radiometry-detector-counts` in Radiometry: from light at the aperture to expected counts in a pixel.
- `optics-roll-rotation` in Optical fields: east and north versus detector axes.
- `records-reporting-law`, `records-covariance` and `records-identities` in Measurements, probability, and records: the reporting event $D$, the offsets $(\xi,\eta)$ and their covariance $C$, indexed by visit $k$ on the record card; calibration revision is one of the record identities.

## Numbers on the graphic

None. The overview and the relay stills print no number, and no brightness ratio is implied by the synthetic frame. The count of four visits is drawn, not printed; a test pins it. The scene parameters (10 pc, a 3 AU orbit at 60 degrees inclination, a disk from 1 to 5 AU) are recorded in the build manifest and set in `tools/explainers/d01_observation_overview.py` (`SCENE`, `FRAME`, `DRAWS`).
