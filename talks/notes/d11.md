## Lesson

The star and the planet both orbit their barycenter on opposite sides, so when the planet moves toward the observer the star moves away, and its radial velocity, defined as positive in recession, is positive.

## What to say

1. The still, top row. We look at the orbit from the east: north is up and the observer is far to the right. The crossed circle is the barycenter. The small dashed circle is the star's orbit, the large solid circle the planet's. The gray arrow r runs from the star to the planet, always through the barycenter. The mass ratio is exaggerated to one half so the star's orbit is visible; for the Earth and Sun it is three millionths.
2. At the first instant, t0, the planet is north of the star and moves toward us. The star is on the other side and moves away. Radial velocity is minus the rate of change of the star's Z, so it is positive: recession. This is quadrature, and the planet is half lit.
3. A quarter period later the planet is nearest the observer, at plus Z, showing us its dark side. Three quarters later it is farthest, at minus Z, and full. Both times the star moves across the line of sight and its radial velocity is zero. Brightness alone does not tell you the sign.
4. The bottom curve is the star's radial velocity over one period, in units of K. It starts at plus K, and half a period later the star approaches at minus K. For the Earth and Sun, K is 0.08946 meters per second, about 9 centimeters per second.
5. The animation. The camera is fixed and time runs at equal steps. The dotted segment at the star is the part of its velocity along the line of sight; the dot on the curve is that part with its sign flipped, so pointing away from us is positive.

For the animation, the one variable is orbital time. Every scale is fixed across frames.

## Assumptions

- Two point masses about their common barycenter, Newtonian, with the systemic velocity and all other velocity corrections removed.
- The observer profile (north, east, toward the observer) and the rule that positive radial velocity means recession are the chapter's proposed conventions, pending at the conventions stage.
- The literature writes the curve with the star's argument of periastron; with the same node and phase, that argument is the planet's plus pi, and rewriting the bracket with it introduces a minus sign, so the literature form gives plus K at t0 only under its own conventions. A formula with an unlabeled omega is not evidence of compatibility.
- The drawn mass ratio is 0.5, not the Earth and Sun's; the planet disk is enlarged; the orbit shape is exact for a circular orbit. The picture is computed from the chapter's reflex relations, not from a library, and it is not a measured system.
