## Lesson

A coronagraph model carries a complex field from plane to plane, and only the
image plane turns it into an intensity; a mirror command therefore changes the
image through the field, so the change in intensity can be negative.

## What to say

1. Each dashed line is a plane, a place along the beam. The glyph on it is the
   hardware there. The square below is an array of numbers standing for the
   quantity on that plane. The first three are what an element applies:
   transmission, OPD, the vortex phase. The next two are what the light
   carries: the field at the Lyot stop and the intensity at the image plane.
   The last is what the detector records.
2. Everything up to the Lyot plane is a complex field. Elements multiply it;
   the gaps marked FT are Fourier transforms; the relay to the DM plane is an
   identity in the model, which applies the DM to the entrance array. The
   square of the modulus is taken at the image plane, and the detector is a
   separate model after that.
3. At the Lyot plane, the vortex has moved the starlight into a ring just
   outside the pupil edge (solid circle), and the stop (dashed circle) blocks
   it. The model's own D-wide array keeps only about 4 percent of the light;
   the thumbnail is evaluated on a wider grid to show where the rest went.
4. The animation walks the planes, then changes one number, the DM ripple
   command c, from 0 to -2 nm; the left panel shows c. Every color scale is
   fixed. At -1 nm the ripple is cancelled and the speckles vanish: the
   intensity change is negative. At -2 nm the speckles are back at the same
   brightness, within 4 percent, but their phase has turned from +pi/2 to
   -pi/2. An intensity map alone barely sees that.
5. The four-panel still shows one command removing a speckle pair and adding
   another, so the difference image carries both signs.
6. The light-at-every-plane still follows the same train and shows the field
   itself: amplitude on top, phase below, before and after each element.
   Every element multiplies the field and every gap is a Fourier transform;
   the one into the Lyot plane is the inverse transform.
   The 1 nm ripple is at most 0.011 rad of phase, too small to see on a full
   cycle, yet it makes the speckles. Before the vortex the focal field is the
   Airy pattern, with rings that alternate in sign; the vortex leaves the
   amplitude alone and winds the phase six times. In the Lyot plane the
   starlight sits outside the pupil edge and the stop blocks it; about 4e-5
   of the light gets through, almost all of it the ripple's light. Only at
   the image plane is the square of the modulus taken.

The animation's variable is a model parameter, not time; say so aloud.

## Assumptions

- Scalar, monochromatic (550 nm) Fraunhofer propagation on a 64 by 64 pupil
  grid and a 48 by 48 focal grid at 0.5 lambda/D; a gray-pixel circular
  aperture, a charge-6 multi-scale vortex (Mawet et al. 2005; Foo et al.
  2005; no page locators checked) and a Lyot stop of 0.8 D.
- Intensities are relative to the unocculted on-axis peak |F[A](0)|^2. The
  focal grid has no sample on the axis; its brightest sample is 0.73 of that
  peak.
- The static wavefront error is a 1 nm cosine ripple of 6 cycles per aperture
  at the DM plane. The DM map is an OPD, not a surface height; at normal
  reflection the surface moves by half the OPD.
- The phase-to-OPD pairing exp(+i 2 pi W/lambda) with an exp(-i 2 pi u x)
  transform is the book's proposed coherent profile, not an adopted standard.
  The model reaches the Lyot plane with an inverse transform, so it does not
  invert the pupil as a physical relay would; viewing direction and parity
  belong to the pending image coordinates decision.
- Detector: 1e7 photons per second in a pixel at the unocculted peak, 1 s,
  quantum efficiency 1, Gaussian approximation to shot noise, 1 electron of
  read noise, detector pixels equal to the focal samples.
- In the light-at-every-plane still, pupil-plane amplitudes are relative to
  the incident amplitude and focal-plane amplitudes to the square root of the
  unocculted peak. The focal pair is the continuous transform of the path's
  entrance field on a 0.25 lambda/D display grid, times the closed-form
  vortex phase; the model's multi-scale vortex does not output that field.
  Phase is drawn only where the intensity exceeds 3e-7 of the reference.
- The train is an illustrative architecture, unfolded with mirrors drawn as
  transmissive elements, not a prescription for any mission. The beam
  envelope is schematic and says nothing about coronagraph performance. A
  backend that stores response tables does not model the intermediate planes.

## Source locators

- `optics-coherent-phase` in Optical fields, image coordinates, and IFS
  products (phase and transform pairing, OPD versus surface displacement).
- `optics-signed-intensity` in the same chapter (signed intensity change,
  composition before sampling).
- Hecht (2017), Optics, 5th edition, Sec. 9.4.2, text before eq. 9.44 (a mirror displaced
  by h at normal incidence changes the path by 2h).
- Mawet et al. (2005), ApJ 633, 1191, and Foo et al. (2005), Opt. Lett. 30,
  3308, for the vortex coronagraph; cited without locators.
