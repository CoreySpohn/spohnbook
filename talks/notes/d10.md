## Lesson

A mirror step changes the path by twice its height, the path change turns the
field arrow, fields add as arrows, and brightness is the squared length of the
sum, so a change to the mirror can make a pixel darker.

## What to say

1. Panel 1: a mirror, seen from the side. Part of its face has moved back by h,
   away from the starlight. The dash-dot line is where the face was.
2. Panel 2: the same mirror. Light that reflects from the moved part travels h
   farther on the way in and h farther on the way out, so the optical path
   difference is 2h. The in and out rays are drawn apart only so both show.
   A mirror command is a surface height; the model wants a path difference.
   Convert once, at the hardware.
3. Panel 3: the light at the marked point is an arrow in the complex plane.
   The path difference turns it by 2 pi W over lambda. In this book's proposed
   convention a positive path difference turns it counterclockwise. The drawn
   step, a sixteenth of a wave, gives an eighth of a wave of path and an
   eighth of a turn.
4. Cross the line: from one mirror point to one image pixel. The pixel's field
   is a sum of many turned arrows, one from every point of the pupil. The
   numbers here are the chapter's worked example, not a result of panel 3.
   Its field is the nominal field E_0,
   0.2, plus the field the mirror change adds, delta E, here -0.1. Arrows add
   head to tail, so the sum is 0.1, the dot on the axis; the arrows are drawn
   off the axis only so they can be seen. The dotted circle shows where the sum
   would end if delta E had another phase: the same change could brighten the
   pixel instead.
5. Panel 5: brightness is the squared length of the arrow. 0.04 before, 0.01
   after. The change is -0.03: negative, while both intensities are positive.
   If a code clips that change at zero before adding it, the pixel stays at
   0.04 and the cancellation is lost.

## Assumptions

- Scalar, monochromatic, coherent light; reflection at normal incidence.
- Panels 1, 2, 4 and 5 are identities under those assumptions. The sense of
  rotation in panel 3, exp(+i 2 pi W/lambda), is the book's proposed pairing,
  not an adopted standard.
- The worked fields E_0 = 0.2 and delta E = -0.1 are the chapter's example
  numbers. They are not computed from the drawn h. The drawn step is
  exaggerated; a deformable mirror moves by nanometers.
- The picture does not show how a particular command maps into a particular
  pixel; the propagated DM-command figure in the same section does that.

## Source locators

- `optics-coherent-phase` and `optics-signed-intensity` in Optical fields,
  image coordinates, and IFS products.
- Hecht (2017), Optics, 5th edition, Sec. 9.4.2, the text before eq. 9.44 (eq. 9.44 itself is the dark-fringe condition): a mirror
  displacement at normal incidence changes the optical path by twice the
  displacement.
