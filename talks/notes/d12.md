## Lesson

A "contrast" is a ratio, and the same planet reads 1e-10, 9.4e-11 or 3.7e-12 depending on what it is divided by, so every ratio needs its denominator and aperture written beside it.

## What to say

1. On the left is one planet, one ten-billionth as bright as its star, at 3.22 lambda/D; on the right is the starlight the coronagraph leaks for a star the size of the Sun at 10 pc. Both images are in one unit: the fraction of the star's incident photons that land in each pixel.
2. Now read the planet four ways. Divided by the host's flux before the coronagraph, it is 1e-10, the textbook ratio. Its brightest pixel divided by the star's brightest pixel without the mask is a little lower, 9.4e-11. That is because the off-axis peak is 94 percent of the unocculted peak. The same brightest pixel read straight off the image unit is 3.7e-12 in the peak 0.25 lambda/D pixel, almost thirty times lower, and it would change with the pixel size. Its sum in the dashed aperture divided by the same sum for a source with planet flux equal to the star's gives back 1e-10.
3. That last one is the yield package's raw contrast normalization. It agrees with the textbook value for a point source only because it divides by the core throughput of that one aperture, and it is the normalization in which the leakage here is 2.4e-14. Compare numbers only when their denominators and apertures match.

## Assumptions

- The planet is an example with band contrast c_b = 1e-10; the package PSFs are averages over 0.9 to 1.1 micron at five wavelengths, so row 4 returns c_b exactly only for a planet whose spectrum has the star's shape. The images are the package's tabulated PSF and leakage map, noiseless.
- The package carries no unocculted PSF, so its off-axis PSF at 20.5 lambda/D, where the core throughput has leveled off, stands in for it, sampled at the planet's pixel phase.
- The definitions are identities. The scalar leakage measure every code should export is a pending decision, and the mapping of these image axes to the sky is pending.
- The chapter lists four denominators; the zero-magnitude reference is not drawn, and the aperture sum is this figure's addition.
