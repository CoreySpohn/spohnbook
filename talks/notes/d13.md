## Lesson

A focal grid in lambda/D is only meaningful with its wavelength: on a fixed
angular grid a sky source stays put while its diffraction grows, and on a
native grid the diffraction keeps its size while the source moves.

## What to say

1. One point source sits at a fixed angle on the sky, about 69 mas from the
   optical axis (the plus sign). Each column is one wavelength: 500, 750 and
   1000 nm. The open circle marks the source, and the dashed circle is the
   first dark ring of its diffraction pattern.
2. Top row, the fixed angular grid. It stores u_ref, the angle in units of
   the reference wavelength over D. Every pixel is 8.6 mas at every
   wavelength. The source stays at u_ref = 4, and the dark ring grows from
   1.22 to 2.44 as the wavelength doubles. The same power spreads over more
   pixels, so the peak dims.
3. Bottom row, the native grid. It stores u_lambda, the angle in units of
   each wavelength over D, with the same spacing in those units. The ring
   stays at 1.22 in every column, but the same source moves toward the axis,
   from 4 to 2, and each pixel grows from 8.6 to 17.2 mas.
4. The consequence: an array plus a pixel scale in lambda/D is ambiguous. The
   record must say which grid it is, the reference wavelength, and which
   diameter D is meant. Converting a fixed-grid scale again with each
   wavelength magnifies the pattern a second time.

## Assumptions

- A clear circular aperture of diameter D = 6 m, so the outer and inscribed
  diameters coincide; monochromatic Fraunhofer imaging with no aberrations
  and no coronagraph.
- The grid definitions are identities from the chapter; the wavelengths,
  diameter, sampling and source angle are one example.
- Pixel values are center-sampled density times the stored cell area, not
  exact pixel integrals. The mapping of these arrays to the sky and the
  detector is pending.

## Source locators

- Chapter clause `optics-angular-grids` in Optical fields, image
  coordinates, and IFS products; `optics-pixel-measure` for the pixel value.
- Hecht (2017), Sec. 10.2.5, eq. 10.58: first dark ring of a circular
  aperture at 1.22 lambda/D.
