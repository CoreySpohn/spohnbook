## Lesson

A positive telescope roll turns the detector under a fixed sky, so the sky turns clockwise in the stored array; an active image rotation changes nothing physical and turns the stored content counterclockwise.

## What to say

1. Top left: the sky chart, with east to the right and north up, a star on the optical axis, and one fixed source up and to the right of it. The square is a 4-by-4 detector; the wedge marks its pixel (0, 0), and the arrows c and r show the directions in which the column and row indices grow. At zero roll the source is in row 2, column 3.
2. Top middle: the telescope rolls by +90 degrees. The sky and the source have not moved; the detector has turned a quarter turn counterclockwise, which the wedge and the arrows show, because a turned square looks the same. The faint dashed arrows are where c and r pointed at zero roll, and the arc theta at the lower right runs from east, the old column direction, to the new one. The source now falls in row 0, column 2.
3. Bottom row: the same detector as stored arrays. From the detector's point of view, the sky has turned clockwise, from (2, 3) to (0, 2).
4. Right column: an active rotation by +90 degrees. Nothing physical turns; the operation moves the stored content counterclockwise, from (2, 3) to (3, 1). The two answers differ, and derotating a frame taken at roll theta is an active rotation by +theta.
5. The animation shows only the roll angle changing, from 0 to +90 degrees, with every scale fixed: the detector turns under the fixed sky, and on the right the source moves clockwise through the stored array to (0, 2).

## Assumptions

- The sky chart is the chapter's worked example, (x_sky, y_sky) = (E, N), drawn with east to the right; a conventional north-up, east-left display reverses only the horizontal display axis, which also reverses the apparent turning sense in the sky-chart view; the stored-array senses are unchanged.
- coronagraphoto's telescope_pa_deg plays the role of theta; despite its name it is a roll, not a position angle. No calibrated zero-point orientation or reflection is applied.
- Pixels follow the proposed profile x = (c - c_x) s, y = (r - c_y) s with the geometric origin 1.5 of a 4-by-4 grid. The image-coordinate profile and the matrix from (E, N) to the sky chart are pending decisions; say so when showing the slide.
- Given the chart, d = R(-theta) s is an identity. Telescope roll is neither an astronomical position angle nor a parallactic angle.
- The pixels follow from geometry alone. The library signs agree: coronagraphoto places sources with the hwoutils matrix ccw_rotation_matrix(-telescope_pa_deg), and hwoutils rotate_image turns an image counterclockwise for a positive angle.
