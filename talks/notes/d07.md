## Lesson

Photoelectrons and dark charge grow with live time, clock-induced charge (CIC) and read noise arrive once per frame at its read, and the reads are why ten 10 s frames take 100.5 s of elapsed time but only 100 s of live time.

## What to say

1. Top of the still: light from the planet and the background enters one of the four aperture pixels, drawn as a charge well. Filled dots are photoelectrons; open dots are dark charge, which appears without any light. Both keep growing for as long as the pixel integrates.
2. To the right, the frame ends: the charge is clocked out, which can add clock-induced charge (the diamond), and the amplifier reads it, which adds read noise (the small Gaussian). That happens once per frame, however long the frame was, and it produces one frame value. The hatched and solid bars under the pixel are the same fills as the live intervals and reads on the timeline below.
3. The middle band is the schedule: ten frames, each a 10 s live interval followed by a 0.05 s read, with frames 3 to 9 left out and the reads drawn wider than to scale. Every read contributes one CIC draw and one read-noise draw, and the recorded result is the sum of the ten frame values.
4. The table is the chapter's worked count example for a four-pixel aperture. The source rates are for the whole aperture and the detector terms are per pixel; the total is the sum of one pixel's output over the four pixels. The first three rows scale with live time, the last two with the number of frames. Read noise adds 160 electrons squared of variance and nothing to the mean. These are expectations, not a random draw.
5. The animation runs the clock through one simulated realization with every scale fixed. The only thing that changes is elapsed time. A read lasts 0.05 s, too short to see between frames, so read the top title instead: elapsed time equals live time plus the number of reads times 0.05 s, and the gap grows by 0.05 s at every read. Each read moves one frame value from the pixels into the record. The bottom strip shows the recorded sum minus its expectation inside the one-standard-deviation band, which widens as the square root of the number of reads.

## Assumptions

- Independent Poisson photoelectrons, dark charge and clock-induced charge; independent Gaussian read noise with one read per frame; unit gain; no saturation, gain fluctuation, correlated reads or charge-transfer effects.
- The mean and variance are identities under those assumptions. The read cadence is the worked example's stated schedule; the acquisition experiment decision that will fix read cadence is pending.
- The drawing does not describe nondestructive sampling, electron multiplication or a particular detector architecture. The reference-case still shows a restricted experiment with the detector-noise terms off and a computed variance; it is not evidence of a stochastic readout.
- In the animation, photoelectrons and dark charge are one Poisson arrival process summed over the four pixels, CIC and read noise are one draw each per read, and the realization was selected as representative: of seeds 0 to 399, seed 16 was chosen because its total ends 0.5 standard deviations below the expectation and no frame value lies more than 1.1 standard deviations from its mean. State this when showing the animation.
