## Lesson

A lenslet IFS sums the entrance light over each lenslet cell and lays every
lenslet's spectrum on the detector as a row of overlapping PSFlets, so the
fluxes extracted from shared pixels share uncertainty.

## What to say

1. Instrument slide. The lenslet array sits in the entrance focal plane. Each
   lenslet collects one square cell of the image, one spaxel, and focuses it
   onto a pinhole. A collimator, a prism and a camera turn every pinhole into
   a short spectrum on the detector, dispersed along detector x. The prism
   bends every wavelength toward its base, the red least, so 713 nm lands
   highest. Neighboring cells give neighboring traces.
2. Origins slide. The entrance panel returns, small, from the previous
   slide, with a box at the center. Three points are easy to conflate and
   are kept apart, each in its own frame. The optical center of the 64-pixel
   entrance cube is (64 - 1)/2 = 31.5. The origin of the lenslet grid, which
   the implementation drawn here puts at 64/2 = 32, is half a cube pixel
   away on each axis. The detector trace origin is where coronachrome
   anchors every trace, the center of the 120-pixel detector, 120/2 = 60;
   with no constant dispersion term, lenslet (0, 0) lands there at the
   660 nm reference wavelength. The chapter sets no convention for this
   point. The reference wavelength is not a bin center, so the trace origin
   sits between the 651 and 663 nm circles. Taking one origin for another
   shifts every spaxel, and every planet position, by half a cube pixel on
   each axis, 1/12 of a lenslet pitch here, or misplaces every bin
   footprint.
3. Animation. One variable changes: the wavelength bin being pointed at. The
   cross, the bin footprint box and the readout step from 605 to 713 nm
   along the trace of lenslet 24, one bin per second. Say plainly that this
   is a scan for explanation, not photons arriving in order: all ten bins
   land at once, and both lenslets' light is on the detector in every frame.
   A thin line joins the lenslet's entrance cell to the footprint in view.
   Then the grid neighbor, lenslet 31, is brought forward: its trace lands
   12 pixels along the trace and 6 pixels across it, and its 605 nm
   footprint is outlined where it overlaps. Image, color scale and axes are
   fixed across frames; only the overlays change.
4. Template slide. A template is the single-wavelength PSFlet, stored about
   its own centroid. Placement adds a separate calibrated correction to the
   geometric trace point, once; the bin footprint is that PSFlet smeared
   over the 2.55 pixels the bin spans. A template that already carries the
   displacement would receive the correction again and be shifted twice.
5. Correlation slide. Neighboring bins of one lenslet share two thirds of
   their pixels. A count in a shared pixel can go to either bin, so raising
   one estimate lowers the other: r = -0.40 to -0.47. Bins two apart are
   positively correlated, r = +0.16 to +0.19, because each is anticorrelated
   with the bin between them. Neighboring lenslets
   share three rows of faint wings; their largest correlation here is
   0.0007, and it grows as traces crowd.

## Assumptions

- A synthetic instrument: a 7-by-7 square grid clocked by arctan(1/2),
  a 174-micrometer lenslet pitch over 13-micrometer pixels, dispersion linear in log
  wavelength, Moffat PSFlets, ten bins from 600 to 720 nm at resolving
  power 50. It is not an instrument prescription or a performance claim.
- The correlation is for unregularized least squares with uniform pixel
  weights. Other weights, priors or regularization change it.
- The centroid correction of about half a pixel to a pixel is illustrative.
- The edge panel shows light past the detector edge from a simulation on a
  wider detector. The current implementation renormalizes clipped
  footprints instead; the chapter treats that as a defect.
- The template profile and the axis order are proposed conventions; the
  half-pixel origin offset and the detector trace origin at the detector
  center are the implementation's current behavior, not adopted
  conventions.

  collimator, prism and camera layout.

- Brandt et al. (2017), Sec. 4, opening paragraph: a microspectrum is the sum
  of monochromatic spots.

  extraction; deconvolving the line-spread function gives a negative
  covariance between neighboring spectral bins; narrowband spots.

  covariance.
