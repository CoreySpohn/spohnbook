## Lesson

A simulated world, an acquired record and an inference model are three different things,
and only the record, never the truth, enters inference and observing policy.

## What to say

1. Left is the simulated world: a star, a planet and the truth record that generated
   them. Truth reaches the detector only as simulated light and as the noise draw, and
   it reaches evaluation directly, along the bottom, for scoring.
2. The middle is what is actually measured. The configured visit is a command that
   points the telescope; the detector returns raw pixels. Visit 1 shows the planet, and
   the sum of the dotted pixels, 84.8 electrons, exceeds the 30 electron threshold, so
   the record keeps D = 1 and the flux. Visit 2 falls below the threshold, so under this
   reporting rule its record keeps only the event D = 0. A successful nondetection is
   data. On each card the threshold L and the noise sigma are fixed settings of the
   experiment, drawn apart from the measured fields.
3. The dashed card is a different experiment that would replace the visit 2 record:
   forced photometry reports 17.7 electrons from the same pixels even though it is below
   the threshold. Choosing between the two is a choice of likelihood.
4. Right is the model and the policy. The inference model holds the hypotheses and is
   updated from the record; the policy is a decision rule that chooses the next visit
   from the posterior only. Evaluation sits below the divider: it alone reads the truth.
   The red crossed arrows are the boundary: no truth and no score reaches inference or
   policy.
5. Second slide: the fit-and-forecast tutorial on a time axis. Cyan points are the five
   acquired epochs; pink is the posterior of the position, and each violin is the whole
   forecast distribution at days 240, 320 and 400. The forecasts are predictions, not
   data. The period ridge makes the day 400 east forecast bimodal, so no single central
   value summarizes it. Gray is the simulated truth, shown only to compare; one of six
   truths falls outside its 90 percent interval, which is expected.

## Assumptions

- The pixels are simulated: a known 20 electron background per pixel, a
  pixel-integrated Gaussian planet image and independent Gaussian noise of 2 electrons
  per pixel. The reduction subtracts the background and sums the 9 pixels within 1.5
  pixels of the fixed target position (the 3 by 3 block), so the flux has a standard
  deviation of 6.0 electrons before any selection.
- The detection rule is the chapter's scalar example: D = 1 precisely when F > L for a
  fixed, known threshold L.
- The information boundary is a proposed convention. The picture does not assert that
  the current tutorial or campaign software enforces every separation it shows.
- The forecast figure reruns the tutorial's own simulation and fit, with the same orbit,
  epochs, random key and samplers. It uses the fitting library's current conventions and
  is an example implementation, not a validation.
